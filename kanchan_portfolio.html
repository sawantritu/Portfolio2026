01 · About

I work between the data and the decision.

A portfolio can show tools. A CV can show job titles. The more useful question is: what happens when Kanchan is given a difficult business question?

I start with the question, not the technology. Then I work backwards. What does the decision-maker need to know? What data can support that decision? Can the data be trusted? What pattern is actually meaningful? And how can the result become something people can understand, use and repeat?

How I Work

Good analytics is a chain of trust.

A sophisticated model built on poorly understood data is still a weak analytical product. My approach therefore puts validation before interpretation and interpretation before communication.

CASE 01 · MARKET RESEARCH

From consumer records to a market story.

CrossTab / Course5 Intelligence · Client environment: Kantar

Market research taught me an important lesson early in my career: the most interesting number is not necessarily the most useful number.

Consumer and retail data can contain thousands of observations, but the stakeholder usually wants to understand behaviour — who is doing what, how segments differ, what changed and what might explain it.

The Challenge

Consumer datasets needed cleansing, validation and analytical structuring before they could support segmentation, pricing, competitive analysis and customer-behaviour questions.

The Approach

SQL, Tableau and Excel were used to prepare the data, investigate patterns, structure segments and turn findings into reporting that could be understood by decision-makers.

The Insight

A segment is useful when it changes how the business understands behaviour. The analytical work therefore focused on distinguishing meaningful differences rather than simply producing more categories.

The Output

Structured analytical outputs and executive reporting that connected consumer evidence with commercial questions.

01 · Before the model

Question first.

What is the business trying to predict, understand or change? A technically impressive model can still answer the wrong question.

02 · During the analysis

Challenge the data.

Missingness, duplicates, inconsistent definitions, unexpected distributions and data leakage can matter more than model complexity.

03 · After the model

Explain the output.

A model should not end with a metric. Stakeholders need to understand uncertainty, important drivers, limitations and how the output can actually be used.

09 · Business Analytics

Numbers become useful when they explain movement.

My commercial analytics experience covers pricing, revenue, customer behaviour, segmentation, forecasting and BI. The objective is always to move from “what happened?” toward “what should we understand about it?”

Illustrative Commercial Trend
Synthetic index, baseline = 100.
SYNTHETIC
How to read it The series is normalized so the baseline is 100. This makes the shape of the trend more important than any absolute value.
What to ask next Where are the inflection points? Which segments contribute to the movement? Is the pattern seasonal, structural or event-driven?
Commercial Question

What changed?

Identify the period where the trend moved, then break the movement down by product, customer, segment, geography or another relevant dimension.

Analytical Question

Why did it change?

Separate correlation from explanation. Look for drivers, mix changes, anomalies and contextual events.

The Business Story

From dashboard to conversation.

I do not see BI as the final destination. A dashboard is useful when it creates a better question, makes an exception visible or helps a stakeholder decide where to investigate.

This is why my experience combines Power BI, Tableau and Excel with SQL, validation, analytical modelling, stakeholder communication and reporting automation.
